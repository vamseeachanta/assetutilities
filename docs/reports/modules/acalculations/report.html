1 Executive Summary

The calculations module is a collection of engineering and lifecycle-cost functions. Analysis is performed to determine how its documented interfaces support a public cost-discounting example, and the key results are summarized below.

  • The public replacement and maintenance examples are calculated separately and combined as an illustration.
    • The combined present cost is 1,320.58 USD at the source example rate of 3.0 % over 5 years. Its equivalent annual cost is 288.35 USD/year. The calculation basis is given in Section 4.
  • Cost timing can be checked using the reported inputs and the cumulative-cost chart.
    • Budget acceptance is not evaluated because no budget or competing design is specified. Structural qualification is not established by this example; the additional evidence is identified in Section 8.

2 Introduction

The implemented calculation areas and the selected workflow are described in this section. Direct function calls are provided for scalar and list calculations; a complete design model is not assembled by the module.

The lifecycle-cost workflow is covered in full from timed cash inputs through present cost to annual equivalent cost. It is selected because its documented interfaces, numerical tests and public reference examples permit a readable arithmetic check without material coefficients or licensed design tables.

2.1 Not covered

The remaining areas are identified without extending the worked-example conclusion to their results.

Riser vibration, array interference, riser dynamics, drilling-riser tension and integrity, wellhead fatigue, steel catenary riser fatigue, tension-leg platform geometry, pipeline resistance and casing ratings are not evaluated here. The polynomial evaluator is a placeholder that returns no numerical value. The source and test inventory is provided in Section 9.2.

Within lifecycle costing, levelized output cost, event-based maintenance, transport, downtime and installed-cost summation are additional callable utilities. Their input contracts are listed in Appendix 10.2; their use in an asset forecast is outside the selected discounting example.

3 Summary of Results

The time-dependent present cost of the combined illustration is presented below. Each point is calculated from the costs incurred up to that year, discounted to the same base date.

The combined present cost is 1,320.58 USD at the source example rate of 3.0 % over 5 years. Its equivalent annual cost is 288.35 USD/year. The replacement payment occurs at the final year end; maintenance occurs at each year end.

Budget acceptance is not evaluated because no budget or competing design is specified. The source examples demonstrate discounting; the combined illustration is not a published asset benchmark.

4 Basis and Criteria

The public source examples and numerical comparison criteria are defined here. NIST Handbook 135, August 2025 edition, is used for discounting theory and example inputs, as identified in Reference 1.

The replacement example uses 1,000.00 USD at the end of year 5. The maintenance example uses 100.00 USD at each year end over the same period. Both use an annual discount rate of 3.0 %. This rate is an example input, not a statement of the current rate required for an investment decision.

Each published example is compared with the module result to the displayed cent. The report tests additionally use a tighter arithmetic tolerance for the hand-worked anchors. This is a reporting-accuracy criterion, without a structural resistance criterion or an investment-acceptance threshold.

No material strength, load factor, design fatigue factor or environmental limit is selected. These quantities remain required inputs for a future structural assessment. No numerical design requirement is taken from a licensed publication.

5 Methodology

The present cost is calculated by discounting each dated payment and summing the resulting base-date values. The equivalent annual cost is calculated from that present cost using the same annual rate and study period.

5.1 Governing equations

The single-payment relation in Reference 1, section 3.2.1, equation (3−6), is applied to each payment.

P = Σ Ct / (1 + r)t

For equal end-of-year maintenance payments, the finite geometric sum is the uniform-present-value relation in section 3.2.2.1, equation (3−7).

Pmaintenance = M [(1 + r)n − 1] / [r (1 + r)n]

The annual-value relation in section 17.4, equation (12−17), is applied to the combined present cost.

A = P r / [1 − (1 + r)−n]

The symbols are defined in Appendix 10.1. The unit terms in the equations follow the cited mathematical definitions; no empirical coefficient is introduced.

5.2 Timing boundary

The example cash flow is represented on an annual timeline. This is an economic model, so no structural geometry, support conditions or mesh is implied.

6 Worked Example

The published replacement and maintenance cases are calculated separately before their cash flows are combined. The combined case is an illustration constructed from those two examples; it is not presented as a third published benchmark.

The input replacement is 1,000.00 USD at the end of year 5. The input maintenance is 100.00 USD at each year end. The annual discount rate is 3.0 %, and the duration is 5 years. The source is the NIST handbook reference.

The replacement present cost returned by the module is 862.61 USD. The maintenance present cost returned by the module is 457.97 USD. Each agrees with its published example to the displayed cent.

The combined present cost returned by the module is 1,320.58 USD. The equivalent annual cost returned by the module is 288.35 USD/year. The replacement contributes 188.35 USD/year and maintenance contributes 100.00 USD/year to that annual equivalent.

No initial purchase, revenue, tax, escalation or residual value is included. An asset-level economic decision is not established by this limited cash-flow illustration.

7 Validation Evidence

The existing repository tests and the additional report checks are distinguished here. The inspected calculation test files contain 150 test functions; this inventory describes the evidence available, without converting the count into a physical-validation claim.

The existing cost tests exercise a timed cost stream, levelized output cost, equivalent annual cost, maintenance cost, transport cost, downtime adjustment and installed-cost addition. Empty cash streams and selected invalid denominators are also covered. The cost-test assertions and the source inventory are located in Section 9.2.

The report tests add hand-worked anchors for replacement present cost, maintenance present cost and combined annual cost. Consistency checks compare the displayed values and chart data with fresh module calls. A rebuild comparison checks the committed document, its change panel and calculation-source digests.

The public NIST examples establish arithmetic agreement for the stated timing and inputs. Field validation, a complete asset cost estimate and qualification of the structural routines are not established by those checks. The inspected cost tests contain illustrative values rather than a measured cost dataset or a whole-asset published benchmark.

8 Limits and Recommendations

The limits affecting interpretation and the required next evidence are stated below. Budget acceptance is not evaluated, and structural qualification is not established by the selected cost example.

  • Restrict this example to deterministic, annually dated cost arithmetic.
    • Supply a complete cost register, a stated currency and price date, consistent escalation and discounting, and comparable service before selecting an asset alternative. This follows the study-basis and discounting treatment in Reference 1, chapters 2 and 3.
  • Check input domains before using the cost utilities.
    • The annual-cost function divides by zero for a zero discount rate or zero duration. Levelized cost divides by zero for zero discounted output and does not reject unequal stream lengths. Event-based maintenance divides by the supplied mean time between failures. Add explicit domain handling and tests before exposing these inputs to an unattended decision tool.
  • Resolve the structural calculation basis before design use.
    • The natural-frequency, cross-flow response and buckle-arrest expressions contain dimensional inconsistencies with their stated output units. Casing collapse includes approximate regime selection. These routines require dimensional correction, edition-specific source checking and reference cases before their outputs are used as design evidence. Source locations are given in Section 9.2.
  • Complete the public interface where numerical behavior is absent.
    • The polynomial evaluator requires a defined coefficient order, units, input domain and value assertions before a polynomial result is reported.

The calculation is repeated using the documented public entry points as follows. Cost lists begin at the base date; the first nonzero payment is therefore preceded by a zero.

from assetutilities.calculations.lifecycle_cost import (
    net_present_value, annual_equivalent_cost,
)
replacement = [0] * 5 + [1000.00]
maintenance = [0] + [100.00] * 5
costs = [a + b for a, b in zip(replacement, maintenance)]
rate = 0.03
present_cost = net_present_value(costs, rate)
annual_cost = annual_equivalent_cost(present_cost, rate, 5)

10.4 Quality pre-flight evidence

The delivered report is checked by the companion report tests. Their scope is numerical consistency, literal hand anchors, calculation-source freshness, output encoding, local references and report structure; those checks do not establish engineering acceptance.

Replacement and maintenance are checked separately against the public examples. Displayed headline values are recalculated from the displayed inputs. The chart values are calculated by the module, without a browser-side cost formula. Source digests cover the calculation files only and are embedded in the document data.

Test results and checks attempted for the delivered revision are stated with the accompanying change description. The rebuild command is provided in Section 9.2.
